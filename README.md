# VeriCite

A browser-based legal citation verification tool built during Cornell Tech’s Milstein Program. VeriCite helps users identify potentially AI-generated citation hallucinations in case law and New York statute citations by resolving them against public legal records.

## What it does

- Extracts case and New York statute citations from natural-language text
- Resolves citations against public legal-record sources
- Flags unresolved references for user review
- Lets users check highlighted webpage text or paste text into an extension popup

## Tech stack

- **Citation extraction:** eyecite
- **Legal-record sources:** CourtListener API, NY Senate Open Legislation API
- **Interface:** Browser extension

## How it works

1. A user highlights text on a webpage or pastes text into the extension popup.
2. VeriCite extracts legal citations from the provided text.
3. It queries public legal-record APIs for corresponding case or statute records.
4. The extension returns resolved records and flags citations it cannot resolve.

## Why we built it

Generative AI can produce legal citations that look credible but refer to nonexistent cases, misstate real authorities, or point to invalid statutory provisions. When those citations reach a filed brief without verification, the consequences can include sanctions, wasted research time, client cost, and reputational damage.

In Mata v. Avianca (S.D.N.Y. 2023), attorneys and their firm were sanctioned $5,000 after submitting nonexistent judicial opinions and fake quotations generated by ChatGPT. The court emphasized the resulting burden on the opposing party and court, along with harm to the profession’s credibility. Opinion and order

More recently, the Ninth Circuit sanctioned attorneys who filed briefs containing nonexistent cases, inaccurate quotations, and misrepresentations associated with generative-AI hallucinations. The court required future filings from the firm to disclose AI use and certify that cited authorities and quotations had been personally reviewed. LNU v. Blanche order

VeriCite is a lightweight first-pass check: it extracts cited cases and New York statutes from text, resolves them against public legal records, and flags references that cannot be resolved. It is designed to keep this verification step within a researcher’s normal browser workflow—not to replace legal research, attorney judgment, or professional citation checking.

## My contribution

In a four-person team, I built the verification pipeline integrating eyecite, CourtListener, and the NY Senate Open Legislation API, and developed the browser-extension workflow for highlighted and pasted text.

## Important limitations

VeriCite is a research prototype. A resolved citation does not establish that the cited authority is current, controlling, accurately characterized, or appropriate for a particular legal question. It should not be treated as legal advice.
